Book miniatures: Hesse, Unterm Rad

Since I currently don’t have access to a decent library, I’ve started re-reading the books I own. I have about 30 books, so depending on how long this situation continues, I might make it through all of them. One perk of this is that I’ll be able to determine which ones I actually want to keep.

I first read Unterm Rad, a slim novel from 1905, when I was 18, in the summer before my final year of high school. The protagonist is Hans Giebenrath, a gifted boy in his early teens who is accepted into an elite, church-run boarding school. While at first he excels among his peers, he soon succumbs to the relentless pressure and exorbitant workload, eventually suffers a nervous breakdown and is expelled from school. It is a burnout avant la lettre.

The whole thing is sentimental as heck and I have no particularly good explanation for why it is still in my possession after almost 15 years, except that it is very slim, and perhaps the following. What is interesting about the story is that it doesn’t end with Hans’ breakdown and return home, as one might expect. We follow him for a while afterwards. After vegetating in his hometown for a few months, it seems as though he is returning to normality and starting a new life: he meets a girl, reunites with an old friend, starts training as a blacksmith. And then he dies. Maybe it is an accident or maybe it is suicide, but even if it is an accident, in the logic of the story it is not accidental. It’s rare to see the story of a failure drag on like this. Usually, a merciful curtain falls once the end is inevitable, but this story just keeps on going in a way that is painful and depressing and makes you lose all hope in this world. I think I’ll keep this one.
